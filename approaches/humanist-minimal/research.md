# Humanist Minimal: research dossier

## 1. Definition

**Humanist Minimal** is minimalism made by hand. It keeps the restraint of modernism (one idea, few elements, lots of empty space, flat colour) but makes every mark look human:
- ink lines that wobble slightly
- organic, rounded paper shapes instead of perfect geometry
- motion that moves like real things do, with weight and spring

Its aim is to feel **clear and warm at the same time**: understood in a second, and felt as made by a person for a person.

It sits between two failure modes:

| Too cold | Humanist Minimal | Too cute |
|---|---|---|
| Swiss-grid corporate, perfect vectors, stock icons | Hand line, organic shapes, one witty idea, real space | Bubbly mascots, bendy-limbed figures, decoration everywhere |

## 2. Lineage

Humanist Minimal combines four traditions. Knowing them helps you judge new work.

### 2.1 Art: drawing with scissors and a single line

| Who / what | When | What we take |
|---|---|---|
| **Henri Matisse**, paper cut-outs (*Jazz*, 1947; *The Snail*, 1953) | 1940s–1954 | Shapes cut directly from painted paper: organic edges, flat colour, no outline needed. Matisse called it drawing with scissors. Source of our **paper** layer. |
| **Jean (Hans) Arp**, biomorphic reliefs | 1910s–1960s | Soft, organic, rounded forms that feel alive. Source of our **organic shapes**. |
| **Alexander Calder**, wire sculptures | 1920s–1930s | Drawing in space with one continuous line, playful and economical. Source of the **continuous ink line**. |
| **Saul Steinberg**, line drawings | 1940s–1990s | The line as thought: wit from the drawing itself, not from decoration. Source of **wit in the idea**. |
| **Ensō** (Zen brush circle) and the Japanese idea of **ma** (meaningful empty space) | traditional | An imperfect circle is more alive than a perfect one; empty space is part of the work. Source of **imperfection** and **space as content**. |

### 2.2 Design: wit and economy

| Who / what | What we take |
|---|---|
| **Paul Rand**, e.g. the IBM "Eye-Bee-M" rebus poster (1981) | Two plain symbols joined into one idea, read instantly. This is our **metaphor recipe**. |
| **Alan Fletcher**, **Bruno Munari** | Wit and play inside strict economy. Design that smiles without decorating. |
| **Dieter Rams**, "less, but better" | Every element must earn its place. |

### 2.3 Typography: letters from the hand

Humanist typefaces come from the shapes a broad-nib pen makes. They have:
- open apertures (the gaps in c, e, a, s)
- slight variation in stroke width
- varied, old-style proportions
- true italics

Key milestones:

| Typeface | Year | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Johnston (Edward Johnston, London Underground) | 1916 | First sans serif based on classical, calligraphic proportions. |
| Gill Sans (Eric Gill) | 1928 | Humanist sans for print and signage. |
| Optima (Hermann Zapf) | 1958 | Flared, stroke-modulated sans: the pen inside a sans. |
| Frutiger (Adrian Frutiger) | 1976 | Developed from the signage for Charles de Gaulle Airport. Open forms built to read at distance and at an angle. |
| Source Sans (Paul D. Hunt, Adobe) | 2012 | Adobe's first open-source family, humanist, made for screens. |

### 2.4 Contemporary brands working in this territory

These are references for the feeling, not for copying:
- **Mailchimp** (rebrand by Collins, 2018): hand-drawn, imperfect illustration with a restrained identity.
- **Dropbox** (rebrand by Collins, 2017): illustration paired with clear, minimal type.
- **Notion**: monochrome ink-line illustration; its art director, Roman Muradov, is an editorial illustrator known for loose ink work.
- **Headspace**: rounded, organic shapes and soft motion that feel calm and approachable.

### 2.5 The counter-example: "Corporate Memphis"

Corporate Memphis began as Alegria, a flat illustration system Buck made for Facebook in 2017. It was copied across the tech industry and is widely criticised as generic: faceless figures with bendy limbs, bright fills and no individuality.

Humanist Minimal shares its friendliness but rejects its methods:

| Corporate Memphis | Humanist Minimal |
|---|---|
| Faceless people with exaggerated limbs | Objects and symbols, rarely people; hands when needed |
| Perfect vector shapes | Hand-wobbled line, organic cut shapes |
| Many bright colours | One Wada combination, plus ink and paper |
| Scenes full of activity | One idea, one focal point, lots of space |
| Decoration as friendliness | Wit in the idea as friendliness |

## 3. Why it works: evidence

| Effect | Finding | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| **The handmade effect** | Across four studies, products described as handmade were seen as more attractive. The main driver was the sense that they symbolically carry *love*; effort, quality and uniqueness were also tested as explanations (Fuchs, Schreier & van Osselaer, *Journal of Marketing* 79(2), 2015). | Visible hand-made marks signal care. That is why the line wobbles and the shapes look cut. |
| **Preference for curves** | People prefer objects with curved contours to sharp-angled ones; the authors suggest sharp angles read as a possible threat (Bar & Neta, *Psychological Science* 17(8), 2006). | Organic shapes and rounded corners make the work feel safe and approachable. |
| **How reliable that preference is** | A meta-analysis of 61 studies found a medium effect (Hedges' g = 0.39). It is reliable but not universal: it depends on presentation time, stimulus type, expertise and task (Chuquichambi et al., *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences* 1518, 2022). | Use curves as the default, but they do not replace a good idea. Sharp details can still be used deliberately (a leaf tip, a beam of light). |
| **Processing fluency** | Things that are easier to perceive tend to be liked more (Reber, Schwarz & Winkielman, *Personality and Social Psychology Review*, 2004). | One idea, one focal point, few elements and lots of space make the work easy to process, and so more likeable. |
| **Aesthetic-usability effect** | Interfaces that look more attractive are perceived as easier to use (Kurosu & Kashimura, 1995). | Craft is not decoration: a warm, beautiful piece is also judged clearer. |
| **Fluid interfaces** | Apple's WWDC 2018 talk *Designing Fluid Interfaces* argues that motion should behave physically (momentum, springs, interruptible) to feel natural and responsive. | Our motion uses springs, not linear timing, and never stops abruptly. |

## 4. What it is and isn't

| It is | It isn't |
|---|---|
| One idea, joined from two plain symbols | A scene or a story with many parts |
| Ink line with a small, steady wobble | Scratchy sketch lines, or perfect vector strokes |
| Organic shapes with rounded corners | Hard geometry, sharp corners, or shapeless blobs |
| Flat Wada colour, black ink, white paper | Gradients, shadows, glows, 3D, many colours |
| 40% or more empty ground | Filling the frame |
| Spring motion that settles | Linear motion, endless jitter, bounces on text |
| Humanist type, sentence case | Handwriting fonts, all-caps headlines |
| Warm, a little witty | Cute, childish, mascots |

## 5. Risks and how we guard against them

| Risk | Guard |
|---|---|
| Looks childish | One witty idea instead of many cute details; restrained type; no faces on objects except eyes as a symbol (H7) |
| Every piece looks the same | Change the metaphor every time; motifs come from the product's own world (`art.json` → `vocabulary`) |
| Low contrast in Wada pairs | Text uses the `text` role; prefer combinations marked AA (see foundations/color) |
| Too much motion | One moving idea per piece; loops of 4 s or less; reduced-motion users see the final frame (`motion.json`) |
| Wobble reads as sloppy | Wobble amplitude is fixed and small (`art.json` → `line.wobble`); lines stay even in weight |
| Shapes go formless | Organic shapes keep a clear silhouette: 6–9 control points and limited irregularity (`art.json` → `shape`) |

## 6. Scorecard

Score each piece 0 (no), 1 (partly) or 2 (yes). 16 or more out of 20 is ready.

1. Says one thing, readable in 2 seconds without the words.
2. The idea joins two plain symbols, or is one strong symbol.
3. At least 40% empty ground, with one focal point.
4. Line is ink black, even, with a small wobble and round ends.
5. Shapes are organic, with rounded corners and a clear silhouette.
6. One Wada combination; the accent is on at most one object.
7. Type is the product's pairing, in sentence case, with at most three sizes.
8. Headline weight sits close to the ink line weight (type and art feel like one hand).
9. Motion (if any) is spring-based, settles on a still frame, and has a reduced-motion fallback.
10. Warm, not cute: wit comes from the idea.
